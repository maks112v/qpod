# Firmware internals

How the scroll-wheel payload hooks the stock Shanling Q2 firmware. For the user-facing behavior it produces, see the [README](../README.md); build and release steps are in [building.md](building.md) and [releasing.md](releasing.md).

`release/bin/demo`, `usr/bin/hciplayer` (the PEQ filter and one branch, [Large MP3s](#large-mp3s)), `usr/bin/bluealsa` (one AAC capability byte), `usr/bin/checkappprocess.sh` (one sleep, [Battery](#battery)), the boot logo, `home_page.bin` (the Coverflow card; the iPod Home list) and the artist page inside `rootfs.squashfs` change, and the stock EQ preset page and the images only the stock EQ pages show (`STOCK_EQ` in `tools/build.py`) are removed. iPod additionally changes the other audited assets in `patch/ipod.json`: local browsing, settings and streaming pages, the equalizer page, the status bar, Now Playing and the theme (`styles/default.bin`). It also removes the 14 carousel images (`CAROUSEL`) and does not add the Coverflow card icons. The build adds `usr/bin/q2video` ([Videos](#videos)) and the new Local Music icons. The kernel is byte-identical, and the builder checks every other inode's name, type, mtime, mode, uid and gid against stock.

## Hooks

Checked MIPS prologues redirect into a payload at `0xb00000`, using the final unused `PT_NULL` program header. demo's `.pdr` section, outside every segment, is zeroed for rootfs space ([building.md](building.md)). Trampolines restore the stock GOT base and resume each original function after its PIC setup. Writable input and position state is mapped at `0xb40000`, a `NOLOAD` section: the segment's memory size covers it, the file does not.

`HOOKS` and the `WM_PAINT_LEAF` in `tools/build.py` are hooked in both variants; `IPOD_HOOKS` and the `IPOD_LEAF` only in iPod, so the Stock build keeps those entry points stock.

Stock V1.32 turns the encoder knob into key events 172/173: `encoderknob_thread_run` (`0x6256a0`) is the sysfs notifier thread, and the rotation handler after it (`0x6258e0`, unnamed in the symbol table) calls `get_direction` (`0x62587c`) and posts them into the main loop: a key-down (`0x110`), a 20 µs `usleep`, then the key-up (`0x114`). The payload moves on the release, at `on_wm_keyup_before_fun`; iPod also sees the press ([iPod row clicks](#ipod-row-clicks)). `get_direction` fires when the knob (200 units a turn) has moved past a threshold since the last tick: stock 20 for a gesture's first tick and 10 after, which the build raises by `WHEEL_TRAVEL` (1.2, to 24 and 12; `WHEEL_THRESHOLDS` in `tools/build.py`) so a small nudge no longer moves a row. These events are not fixed physical detents. Below, a tick is one such event and a step is a row move the payload makes.

## Supported screens and panes

Navigation requires a supported top-window name from `patch/contexts.inc`, screen-on and no lock/test/guide/power-off/USB-link/Bluetooth-receive screen, and a navigable pane: a vertical `scroll_view`, a `table_client`, a `slide_menu` or a window flagged `BUTTONS`, whose buttons are its rows and which never scrolls ([ipod.md](ipod.md#pop-ups)). Horizontal and page-snapping scroll views are not candidates, so they cannot make a page look like it has two panes. When two panes are visible and neither owns the selection, the first wheel turn chooses the first populated pane in UI order. Later turns follow the selected pane, and tapping a row switches ownership. If both panes already hold a selection, the wheel event is left alone. Only active `pages` children are searched. The bounded walk collects at most 512 targets in a non-virtual list; virtual music tables navigate by total logical row count instead.

A native click chooses its pane from the actual target before walking up to the nearest collected ancestor. A successful selection clears the other pane’s selection and invalidates both panes, so the drawn selection, wheel and centre follow the row that owns the tap. Hidden, disabled and inactive-page panes remain excluded.

An unowned pair prefers a pane with clickable rows over an empty placeholder. If the owning pane loses all its rows while the other pane has rows, the next wheel event transfers ownership. Text-only panes retain their pixel-scroll fallback when neither pane has clickable rows.

The folder-scan table includes the enabled, visible `btn_startscan` footer as one extra logical selection after the native rows. The table's physical row count, scrollbar and scroll bounds remain native. Selecting the footer stops table momentum and draws its outline at the button's own origin; Centre dispatches its native scan callback.

## Selection

Selection is stored in widget-owned integer properties on the navigation surface, independently of AWTK's focused flag. The drawn selection (outline or bar) and centre action resolve that same logical selection against the current entries. Centre dispatches a synchronous native `EVT_CLICK`, so a queued click cannot hit a row rebound between selection and delivery. It never dereferences the target after delivery.

## Wheel movement

Lists with at most `SHORT_LIST_MAX` (16) logical entries always move one row per step. Virtual tables use their total logical count, not the recycled row pool size. Longer lists use a continuous ramp: a same-direction tick spaced at most `WHEEL_RUN_MS` (140 ms) from the previous one extends a spin run, and the step grows with the run's duration, capped at `WHEEL_MAX_STEP` (8) rows. It is a duration ramp, not a measure of the wheel's angular speed. `ramp()` takes the curve from its caller:

| Caller                                                             | One row while the run is under | Then                                      | Eight rows from |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Stock build and iPod's pixel-scroll fallback                        | `WHEEL_RAMP_MS` (100 ms)       | `1 + run / WHEEL_RAMP_MS`                 | 700 ms          |
| iPod row lists and button dialogs                                  | `LIST_FIRST_MS` (300 ms)       | `2 + (run - 300) / LIST_RAMP_MS` (200 ms) | 1500 ms         |

The pixel-scroll fallback is a pane without clickable rows, which scrolls `RING_STEP` pixels per step. A pause, reversal, touch, native click, centre press, list boundary or rejected navigation resets the run. Acceleration belongs to the current window, pane and browsing scope; a list resize also resets it.

iPod row lists and button dialogs drop an overshoot tick. `ramp()` places every accepted tick in its run (`wheel_tick`): the first after a stall longer than `OVERSHOOT_MAX_MS` (140 ms), a reversal, or a change of window, pane, scope or context; a second one that comes `OVERSHOOT_MIN_MS` (80 ms) to `OVERSHOOT_MAX_MS` after the first; or any other. `ringnav()` drops that second tick, taking it for the detent a single-row turn slipped past: a second tick sooner than that is a spin and moves, a later one is a first tick of its own, and the third and later ticks always move. The dropped tick still times the ramp, stops momentum, wakes the scrollbar, repaints and is consumed; it moves and sounds nothing, and no timer makes up for it later. Touch, a native click, any button, a list end, rejected or unavailable navigation and a change of row count end the run, so the next tick is a first one; a surface recreated at the same address has no `_ringnav_count` property, which `load()` treats as a resize. A tick that only reveals the selection and a tick against an end are never dropped. Carousels (the Stock build's Home, Coverflow's covers), other slide menus, the pixel-scroll fallback and the volume take every tick, as does the Stock build. Both bounds are constants in `patch/navigation.c`.

Wheel movement cancels existing momentum and uses the stock `scroll_view_set_offset` / `table_client_set_yoffset` setters immediately, reloading table row references after synchronous rebinding. Each wheel step then dispatches `EVT_SCROLL_END` (`0x14f`), which touch momentum sends when it settles: the setters send only `0x14e`, which the album page takes as pause, and stock lists queue their visible covers on scroll end (album page `0x4a440c`). Remembered-position restoration retains its separate glide. This follows the row-based approach of Rockbox's [H2/Eros Q wheel mapping](https://github.com/Rockbox/rockbox/blob/master/apps/keymaps/keymap-erosq.c) and [list implementation](https://github.com/Rockbox/rockbox/blob/master/apps/gui/list.c).

Selected and restored rows use a `SCROLL_MARGIN` of 12 pixels above and below, reduced to half the spare viewport height in tight lists and clamped at content boundaries. Rows taller than the viewport align their top edge when selected or restored, keeping their title visible.

## List ends

The local file and music lists carry over at their ends; `patch/contexts.inc` marks those audited row lists with `ring`, while grids such as `album_page`, settings menus, dynamic pages, the Stock build's Home carousel and the iPod Home list keep hard ends. The first tick past an end nudges only the drawn selection by `BUMP_PX` for `BUMP_MS`, without moving the viewport. Further ticks hard-stop there, bumping again, while the wheel keeps turning: each one re-arms. Only the first same-direction tick at least `EDGE_PAUSE_MS` (300 ms) after the last stopped one selects the opposite end of the same list and reveals it immediately. The existing boundary rule already reset the spin run, so the wrap lands at one row per tick. The arm is cleared by a move off the boundary row, a reversal, touch, a native click or a centre press.

iPod's overshoot filter never drops a tick against an end: every tick bumps and re-arms, so the pause is measured between ticks. Reaching an end, a bump and the wrap end the run and leave no speed; the next tick is a first one.

## Key Tone

The click is the MCU's buzzer, the device's own speaker: `buzzeer_switch` (`0x4f3cc8`) runs `system("cmd_mcu write_str buzzer")` when the byte `g_keytone_flag` (`0xa38c31`, the Key Tone setting) is set, and nothing else; stock sends it whatever plays and whatever is plugged in. Both builds hook it (`ringnav_buzzer`, stock resumed at `0x4f3cd4`), so every click, stock's three call sites and the iPod row click below, passes one gate: none while music plays (`mclGetPlayStatus`, `0x5ac110`, reads `0xa3beac`: 1 stopped, 2 playing, 3 paused, as `mclStop`, `mclSetResume` and `mclSetPause` store it), and none while headphones or another output listen: either jack (`g_po_status` `0xa38af3`, 3.5 mm, GPIO `PA07`; `g_bal_status` `0xa38af2`, 4.4 mm, `PA08`; 1 plugged, kept by `check_headset_status` at `0x4e8e14`) or an output way other than the DAC (`mclGetOutputWay`, `0x5ac484`: 1 Bluetooth, 2 USB DAC, as `config_outputchannel` sets it). Otherwise stock runs, so with nothing plugged in and the music stopped or paused the buzzer clicks as before, and Key Tone off is still silent. There is no headphone click: a paused hciplayer keeps its PCM (`hw:1,0`, or `bluealsa`, one client per PCM, no `dmix`) open and stock's pause mutes the DAC (`mclSetMute`, `player_play_pause` `0x5157d8`), so a click has no free output; silence is the fallback, never the speaker.

### iPod row clicks

Stock clicks on the press. `on_wm_keydown_before_fun` (`0x4e8424`) calls `buzzeer_switch` (the exported spelling; its only three call sites). A latch (`0xa37c90`), set after the call and cleared by `on_wm_keyup_before_fun`, keeps it to one click a press. Every wheel tick is a press and a release, so stock clicks on every tick, whether or not a row moves.

iPod hooks that callback (`ringnav_keydown`, stock resumed at `0x4e8430`). For a wheel press that the release will take as row navigation it saves `g_keytone_flag`, clears it, runs the whole stock body and restores the byte before returning stock's result: the power timer, the lock gates and both latches run as stock, and only the click is missing. Besides `buzzeer_switch`, the byte is read by `config_init` and by the System settings page's Key Tone row and its click handler (`0x4cc0a0`, `0x4cc2dc`), all on the UI thread, and the stock body calls nothing back into the UI, so nothing else sees it cleared. Whether a press is such a press is decided from the widget tree alone (screen usable, a `contexts.inc` window, a pane that is not a `slide_menu` and holds a clickable row); nothing is loaded, selected or restored. Every other press runs stock unchanged: the buttons, the volume, carousels and other slide menus, the pixel-scroll fallback, and the wheel while the screen is off or locked.

The press leaves its key in `tone_key`. Every key-down rewrites it and every key-up clears it, so a dropped release cannot carry it into a later input. On the release, `ringnav()` calls `buzzeer_switch(1)` once when that tick's step changes the selected row, with the setting as it is then: one click for a step of any size and for a wrap, and none for a dropped overshoot tick, a hard end, a bump, a rejected tick (a pointer down, a window animation, stock's wheel lockout after a button) or a release whose press stock already clicked. If the page stops being a list between the press and the release, that tick is silent and the next press is stock's again. The call is in the wheel path, not in `select()` or the paint hooks, which also run for restoration, touch and repaints. The Stock build has no key-down hook and clicks as stock.

## Scrollbar

Wheel motion also wakes the native scrollbar of the controlled surface. `native_scrollbar()` finds the surface's `scroll_bar_m` sibling under its `list_view` or `table_view` parent and calls the stock `scroll_bar_scroll_to` with the bar's current value; the stock bar keeps ownership of the thumb value and runs its own show, wait and fade lifecycle. An initially transparent bar is still woken, and a surface without a native bar is unchanged. The payload retains no bar pointer and no fade timer, so page destruction and widget recycling need no extra handling. The end bump's `fx_token` widget property and its `fx_expire` timer remain separate from scrollbar visibility: the token rejects a recycled surface, and the timer repaints the selection at the `BUMP_MS` (120 ms) deadline.

## Slider settings

On `backlight_page`, `maxvol_page`, `bootvol_page` and `balance_page`, wheel releases dispatch a synchronous native click to `img_add` or `img_dec`, retaining stock bounds, labels and configuration writes. Quick Settings (`statusbar_dialog`) changes `slider_backlight` by one within its live min/max bounds; the slider's value setter emits `EVT_VALUE_CHANGED`, whose stock callback calls `config_lightness`. Hidden or disabled controls, window animations and pressed pointers consume the wheel without changing playback volume. The volume dialog retains stock wheel handling.

## Centre button

On `manualtime_page` and `sleepshutdown_page`, the wheel steps the outlined text selector through its live options with the native `text_selector_set_selected_index`, retaining the stock value-change callbacks and date validation. A delayed single Centre advances through the fields, switching Date to Time as needed, then selects OK. Centre on OK dispatches the native confirmation click. The wheel on OK returns to the last field when turning back, or the first field when turning forward. Double-Centre keeps the stock screen-off action. Touch, other keys, page destruction and invalid input gates cancel pending advancement.

A centre release arms a UI timer for `DOUBLE_CLICK_MS` (200 ms). On lists, a second release before expiry on the same live selection cancels confirmation and passes through the stock downstream key-up handler to turn the screen off; a single release dispatches exactly one synchronous click at expiry. On Now Playing, the single-release callback advances `MCL_MODE` through Order, Repeat One, Shuffle and Repeat All with `config_playmode`, while the double release takes the same screen-off path.

When the screen is off or locked, Centre requires two releases no more than 500 ms apart by default (`WAKE_DOUBLE_CLICK_MS`). This separate window allows for the screen-off loop's 40 ms input delay, or 100 ms in Low power mode; screen-on double-click actions keep their 200 ms window. The first stays dark and does not activate the page underneath. The second uses stock `on_wm_keyup_fun` to wake an off screen, then `navigator_back` to unlock if needed; `poweroff_page`'s close callback clears `g_lockscreen_pageflag`, while the saved lock setting is unchanged. System settings → Power management → Wake switches between Double click and Single click in both builds (`Q2POD SINGLEWAKE` in `config.ini`, missing or invalid values default to 0). Single click wakes and unlocks on the first release. Changing this setting or releasing another key cancels a pending first click.

Stock's key-up filter rejects wheel releases while a centre-key lockout (`KEY_LOCKOUT`, `0xa37c89`) is non-zero: the centre key sets it to 8 and `on_wm_timer_setup_state` counts it down every 100 ms, so a page opened by the centre button would ignore the wheel for up to 800 ms. The click clears it just before dispatch, so the opened page scrolls at once; a double press keeps stock's lockout. Touch, wheel input and invalid navigation state cancel pending confirmation. The timer resolves the target from the live menu and requires the original window, surface, content scope, logical selection, row count and row-text identity to match. Widget-owned tokens also reject reused window/surface addresses, and an ordinary list requires the armed row widget's own token; virtual tables resolve recycled pool widgets by logical index. An overdue confirmation runs before the power and lock gates that follow it. No delayed row pointer is retained; pending state clears before dispatch. Timer allocation failure consumes the press without activating anything.

## Queue menu

Centre holds never enter stock's shutdown confirmation. On Now Playing, a hold dispatches a click to the hidden, enabled `img_more` widget, using its stock callback and queue context to open the player menu. Both builds hide and move the redundant on-screen button offscreen in `playing_page.bin`. The shortcut requires the normal power and lock gates, no window animation and no pressed pointer. Its matching release is swallowed so it does not also cycle the bottom control or activate a menu row. Where no player or row actions apply, the hook consumes the hold and marks its release to be swallowed. System settings → Power management → Shut down calls the stock Centre-hold trampoline with a synthetic key event, retaining its power-state guards and confirmation before shutdown.

Holding Centre (key 218) fires the stock long press after 1000 ms. `ringnav_keylong` takes it only with the navigation gates, AirPlay off (`airplayGetFlag() != 2`), no batch-select mode (`g_navbar_status`) and the row a centre press would open, on a `CTX_LOCAL`/`CTX_FOLDER` page, an artist's page (`artistinfo_page`, whose tabs fill the showlist) included. There the row's class is `g_local_classinfo_save+0`, which its tabs' `load_localartist_list` sets (`0xff07` songs, `0xff01` albums); `g_class_type` keeps the class the page opened or was last returned to with. The page's row count must equal `deque_size(*p_deque_showlist)`: these lists bind row _i_ to showlist record _i_ (Local Songs `table_row_of` → `deque_at` at `0x4a8838`), and a grid or extra clickable row fails the check. Other keys tail-call the stock body unless the Play/Pause shortcut applies.

Holding Play/Pause (key 171) uses the same long-key callback to switch to `playing_page` with the stock no-restart context `{0, 0, 0xff, 2}`. Both holds store their key record's u64 press time (record `+8`; 16 records of `0x18` from `+0x30`), and the key-up hook swallows the matching release so Centre does not also activate a row and Play/Pause does not toggle playback. AWTK can drop a release when it aborts pressed keys on a window change; a later press has a new time, so a stale latch cannot eat it.

The target is the showlist index plus hashes of the record strings (`+8` name, `+0xc` path, `+0x10`, `+0x14` album, `+0x18` artist) and of the browsing state (`g_folder_path`, `g_class_type`, `g_local_classinfo_save`, `g_artist_type`); the dialog open and the action both re-check it. A type-8 record is one song. In `g_class_type` `0xf003` a row is an album: `getMusicByAlbum(id == -1 ? NULL : album)`, as `load_album_detaillist` → `load_localclass_list(0xff10)` does. In `0xff01` (an artist's albums) it is `getMusicByAlbumAndAlbumSonger` when `g_artist_type == 1`, otherwise `getMusicByAlbumAndSonger`, with `(album, classinfo[0xa] ? NULL : artist, id == -2)` (`0xff11`). A composer's (`0xff02`) or genre's (`0xff03`) albums use `getMusicByAlbumAndComposer`/`getMusicByAlbumAndGenre` with `+0x20` composer or `+0x1c` genre and `classinfo[0xb]`/`[0xc]`. An artist row (`0xf004`) is `getMusicBySonger(+0x18)`, or `getMusicByAlbumArtist` when `g_artist_type == 1`; a composer (`0xf005`) `getMusicByComposer(+0x20)`, a genre (`0xf006`) `getMusicByGenre(+0x1c)`; an id of -1 (Unknown) passes NULL. These are the expansions `batch_add_file` (`0x4f4be8`) runs for each class. A folder row (type 4) calls `toolsLoadDirectory(g_folder_path + "/" + name)` like `folder_enter`. Queries fill `tools_pdeq_directory`; its type-8 records join in order and the staging deque is restored.

Coverflow album holds resolve the live `SLIDE_INDEX` against the current sorted album deque, without opening its tracks. Sort, Refresh, preparation, empty screens and pending screen changes are excluded. Both the deferred open and action revalidate the selected record. Play next, Add to queue and Shuffle use `coverflow_album_tracks`, shared with opening an album, so disc/track/path/CUE ordering and staging restoration are identical. Go to artist is available when the album has an artist; playlist actions remain omitted.

The menu is the stock `dialog/sortselect_dialog`: its init is synchronous and clears `to_modal`, so `navigator_to` returns with it on top, and it is already a navigation context. The payload replaces its five sort rows with the row's own, titles it with the item, and swaps its key-up (`0x49f3e0`) and back arrow (`0x49f3c0`) handlers, both of which set `g_sort_changeflag`, which makes Local Songs reload and scroll to the top on return. The open is deferred out of the long-press dispatch and the action out of the row click; the result is a stock `dialog/msginfo_dialog` toast (`{1, 2000, text}`).

The rows are stock actions. Every row has Play next and Add to queue. A song (and a Coverflow track) adds or removes a favourite, labelled from `checkFavExist`, as Now Playing's heart (`manage_myfav`): Add selects the row alone in the batch record (`batch_init_selectrecord(rows)`, `batch_set_selectitem(index)`, as `player_addplayList` does) and runs batch-select's Add to My Fav, `batch_add_file(class, 0xf00a, list, *p_vector_select_record, 0)`, which reads a folder file's tags first; Coverflow passes its own deque as class `0xf001`. Remove is `deleteMusicFromFav`, and on My Fav (`0xf00a`) it sets `g_delete_flag` so the list reloads, as the heart does. Albums, artists, composers, genres and folders get Shuffle instead: their songs, the play mode saved as shuffle, from a random song, as Shuffle Songs. Everything but Coverflow gets Add to playlist: the row selected the same way, then `localmusic/playlist_page` with context `1 << 16 | class`, the page's add mode, whose pick runs `batch_add_file(class, 0xf00b, showlist, ...)` for the playlist and shows its own toast. A song gets Go to album and a song or a row of all albums Go to artist, when the record has one: Now Playing More's Album info (`0x5283b0`: `classinfo` `+0` = `0xff10`, `+9` = 0, `+0xd` album, `+0x10d` artist, then `playerjumpinfo_page`) and Artist info (`localmusic/artistinfo_page` with `{class, record}`). Both pages reload from `g_local_classinfo_save`, and so do the pages underneath when they come back, so the payload copies it before and writes it back on the opened page's `EVT_WINDOW_CLOSE`. Go to album is left out on an album's own list (`0xff1x`) and either is left out while its page is already open (`navigator_window_is_exist`): both keep single-instance state, which stock's More closes first. Now Playing keeps its stock heart and More page; Photos, Books and Videos have no menu.

The queue is `*mcl_pdeqplaylist` (`0xa269a4`) with static state at `0xa3bdb0` (shuffle pool, a deque of unplayed indices), `0xa3be90` (shuffle previous index), `0xa3be94`/`0xa3be98` (gapless preloaded index/flag), `0xa3bea0` (queue class), `0xa3bea4` (position), `0xa3bea8` (mode) and `0xa3beb4` (player socket). Only a local queue (class 1 or `0xfxxx`) grows. An empty queue is loaded by `mclLoadPlayList(tracks, 0, class)`, which does not start playback. Otherwise Add appends with `_deque_push_back` and Play next rebuilds the deque with the tracks at position+1 (`deque_clear` + `deque_assign`, as stock loads it). Pool entries and the previous index at or after the insert shift, and the new indices join the pool. Gapless preloads only pos+1 (modes 0 and 3), so a set preload flag is closed exactly when the tracks land there, with the `mclSetPlayMode` sequence (`{mcl-closegapless\null}` to the socket, flag -1). In shuffle the checked call at `0x5addf0` in `mclNextSong` (`bal 0x5a8120`, delay slot `a0 = 1`) becomes `jal ringnav_shuffle`: it runs the stock pick, then makes the first Play next track the position once, if the queue still holds it there. Repeat-one repeats the current track by design.

## Home carousel

iPod has no carousel: Home is an ordinary list ([ipod.md](ipod.md#home)), with hard ends. The Stock build's carousel uses `HOME_SLIDE_MS` (200 ms) for isolated ticks and `HOME_FAST_SLIDE_MS` (120 ms) when consecutive same-direction ticks arrive at most `HOME_FAST_WINDOW_MS` (200 ms) apart. There is no extra home input gate; stock filtering and audible ticks stay stock-controlled. Each accepted tick retargets one animator from its live offset, extending the intended destination by one icon. Reversal discards that destination and heads toward the adjacent card in the new direction at normal speed. Stock completion handles wrapping and card selection; centre confirmation follows the intended final icon. Touch, native clicks, centre presses, leaving home and unavailable navigation reset speed tracking; wheel input cancels pending centre confirmation. Allocation failure settles on the requested icon without retaining an animator.

## Sorting

Every name-sorted library list (artists, album artists, composers, genres, albums and songs: the `group by … COLLATE NOCASE` queries and `sort_first_alphabet`) is sorted in C, not SQL: `toolsQueryDbTable(…, 1)` and `toolsQueryDbTableAndSort` mode 0 pass `algo_sort_if` one of two comparators, `0x5ba658` or, with the flag at `0xa3bf4d` clear, the pinyin one `0x5b9d40`. The `ORDER BY` queries sort by time, sample rate or id, and folders sort by file name in `toolsLoadDirectory`'s own comparators. Both name comparators `snprintf` the row's `+0x10` name into a local buffer and `bal toolsTrimLeft` on it, falling back to the file name when it is empty. Those four calls (`SORT_TRIMS`) become `jal ringnav_sort_key`, which trims and then drops a leading "The ", "A " or "An " (any case) when a name follows, as Apple does; the record is untouched. "Them", "Anthrax" and a bare "The" keep their place, and equal keys ("The Cure", "Cure") stay as stock's `algo_sort_if` leaves them. Coverflow follows this order. Stock has no A–Z jump index; on a `CTX_LOCAL` page iPod's fast-scroll letter skips the same article.

## Unknown rows

`load_localclass_list` (`0x5088cc`) runs the class's query into `tools_pdeq_directory`, `deque_assign`s it to `*p_deque_showlist`, clears the staging deque and returns `deque_size(*p_deque_showlist)`, which its callers take as the row count. For `0xf003`–`0xf006` the query is `getAllAlbum`, `getAllArtist` (`GetAllAlbumArtist` when `g_artist_type == 1`), `getAllComposer` or `getAllGenre`, and each appends an Unknown row (id -1) whenever the list has any rows (`getAllArtist` `0x4fd4c4`: type 4, the "Unknown Artist" string), even when every song carries the tag. `ringnav_localclass` runs stock, and when that class's last row has id -1 it runs the query the row's press would run (`class_query`, shared with the [Queue menu](#queue-menu): `getMusicBySonger(NULL)` and its kin, `where songer=""`). With no song it `deque_pop_back`s the row and returns one less. The staging deque is cleared again, as stock leaves it. Coverflow builds its own album list and drops an empty Unknown card the same way (`drop_unknown`).

## Coverflow depth

Coverflow draws its covers in software, after Rockbox PictureFlow's renderer: the selected cover faces the viewer at its native 160 pixels, two neighbours a side are turned 60 degrees inward, pushed back and dimmed, and each has a reflection. `patch/coverflow.c` owns the frame and textures, with the shared integer renderer in `patch/coverflow_render.inc`; the stock `slide_menu` stays the navigation and animation controller, so the wheel (one retargeted animator, 200 ms or 120 ms fast, reversal), wrapping, centre, the saved album and track positions and the Refresh card all work as before. The covers are wheel-only: the `slide_menu` is insensitive, so touch neither drags nor taps them. Tunables are `CF_*` in `patch/offsets.inc`; adjust them together.

**Layout.** With depth the `slide_menu` spans the frame, 375 × 210 (`CF_VIEW_W` × `CF_VIEW_H`) at the top of the page, so every animator step invalidates all of it. Its items stay `h` square (210), and `slide_menu_set_spacer` (a store at `+0x84` and an invalidate) sets the spacer to `CF_STRIDE - 210`, so the stride that stock scroll, completion and ringnav's wheel steps use (item width plus spacer) is `CF_STRIDE` (110 px). The children are `image` widgets with no image, so stock paints nothing under the frame. The album and artist captions sit under the frame at `CF_TEXT_Y` in both builds and change on `EVT_VALUE_CHANGED`, when a move commits, never mid-move.

**Position.** `ringnav_paint`, the border hook both builds install, calls `coverflow_paint` for every widget after stock has painted it; only Coverflow's `slide_menu` on the covers screen draws. The visual position is the index less the live offset over the stride (stock completion commits `index - offset / stride`): centre album `c` and `frac`, 16.16, in `[-1/2, 1/2)`. Every cover's rotation, place, depth, brightness and reflection come from that one continuous value, so an incoming side cover unfolds into the centre as the outgoing one turns away.

**Projection.** All integer (the stock build is `-mfp64`, and integer arithmetic is also cheaper here). A cover `t` albums from the position gets a rotation that grows linearly to `CF_ANGLE` (60 degrees, a 17-entry sine table) at one album, a centre `x` that reaches `CF_X1` then steps `CF_XS` per album (quadratic up to one album, so the two covers turning mid-step keep clear of each other), a depth that reaches `CF_Z1` then `CF_ZS` per album, and a brightness of 256 at the centre, `CF_BRIGHT1` (75%) one out, `CF_BRIGHT2` (50%) two out and 0 three out, so covers fade in and out at the edge of reach. Left covers mirror right ones about the centre line (between columns 186 and 187). For each screen column the renderer inverts the perspective (eye `CF_EYE` px away) to the cover column under it and the cover's projected height there, then steps down the texture column, top and bottom rows weighted by coverage. At rest the selected cover is its texture pixel for pixel. Below each cover its reflection is the column mirrored, `CF_REFLECT` rows at native size scaled by perspective, from `CF_REFLECT_TOP` (25%) of its brightness down to nothing: no blur or glow.

**Compositing.** The frame is drawn nearest cover first: each pixel's alpha byte holds the coverage so far and every layer fills only what is left, which composites as drawing back to front would, and per column a span of rows known to be opaque is skipped outright, so hidden parts of side covers cost nothing. A last pass makes every pixel opaque over black. The hit test walks the same order, nearest first, over cover bodies only.

**Frame and textures.** One `bitmap_create_ex(375, 210, 1500, 1)` (RGBA8888; `bitmap_t` is 0x48 bytes, flags `u16 @0xc`, format `u16 @0xe`, buffer `@0x14`) is created with the covers and marked `BITMAP_FLAG_OPAQUE` (bit 0, as `bitmap_init_from_rgba` sets it for opaque images); `bitmap_lock_buffer_for_write`/`bitmap_unlock_buffer` bracket each render and `canvas_draw_image(canvas, bitmap, src, dst)` (`0x635254`, `dst` relative to the canvas origin, which is the `slide_menu`'s) draws it 1:1. It is re-rendered only when the position or a texture changes; a repaint in place only draws. Textures come from the existing JPEG cache: `widget_load_image`, `bitmap_lock_buffer_for_read` and `bitmap_unlock_buffer` read the decoded image (the stock loader, `stb_load_image` called with RGBA8888 for both formats at `0x6810cc`, never premultiplies), its centred square is sampled to 160 × 160 over black into the payload's own buffer, and the load is dropped at once with `widget_unload_image`, as stock cover rows do; the image manager's copy is only read. Seven slots (`CF_RING`) hold the covers within three albums of the position; as movement crosses an album the one leaving is dropped and the one entering is decoded. A small library repeats round the ring as the `slide_menu` wraps, each album decoded once. Missing art and the Refresh card use `default_album_big`, decoded once and left in the manager's cache as a theme image. The frame and all eight textures (about 1.1 MB) are allocated together when the covers are built; if either fails, Coverflow keeps the flat stock covers exactly as before. Refresh and closing the page destroy the frame and free the textures.

**Checks.** `test/coverflow.py` builds the renderer on the host and checks the selected cover pixel for pixel, that nothing is drawn outside the frame, mirror symmetry at rest and mid-turn, brightness one and two out, the reflection's rows and fade, that a small step changes little (including where the centre album changes hands), the texture window's loads and releases, the centre click, one- and two-album libraries, missing art, Refresh, return from tracks and the allocation-failure fallback; `--captures DIR` writes the renderer's frames at rest and a quarter, half and three-quarter turn as PNGs. `test/patch.py` runs the MIPS payload's renderer against the host build byte for byte, and the page's paint, centre click and fallback, and prints the payload's instructions per frame (about 3.5 million for a frame mid-turn under the mocks): a relative measure, not a frame time.

## Position memory

Position memory is a bounded 64-entry table keyed by the audited top-window context in `patch/contexts.inc` and a content fingerprint. Selecting a row updates its entry and moves it to the front; a full table evicts the least recently selected entry. Restoration alone does not promote an entry.

Local folders use the full bounded `g_folder_path`; local music lists use the stock browsing class, saved query and artist/album modes (`g_class_type`, `g_local_classinfo_save`, `g_artist_type`, `album_modetype`). These are the inputs used by the stock folder and local-list loaders, not playback metadata. The builder verifies each object's size against the stock ELF. A changed fingerprint resets selection even if a surviving surface has the same row count, then restores the matching recent position and reveals it, or keeps the stock viewport if none is remembered. Fixed settings menus can restore by window name alone; multi-pane and other dynamic pages without an audited content identity keep their live widget selection but do not restore across recreation.

Memory lasts until power-off, with no filesystem writes. The table occupies 1,280 bytes. Ordinary paints do not search it; repeated selections in the current scope match its first entry and shift nothing. Lookup and promotion are bounded by `POS_MEM` (64).

Non-virtual lists also remember hashes of the selected row's first two text values, read through the stock `widget_get_text` UTF-32 accessor. A matching second text outranks proximity; equal matches choose the occurrence nearest the remembered index. Rows without text fall back to the index within the same content scope. The stock rows expose no stable item id. Both scroll views and virtual music tables protect an interrupted recall glide from silently replacing the remembered selection with a currently visible row. A real tap cancels the glide and selects its target; a wheel step advances from the remembered logical row. Pointer-down and synchronous click delivery load rows without recall, so native touch sees the widget it pressed and a rebuilt list cannot move or rebind that row first. Synchronous table rebinds discard the old row-pool snapshot before resolving the selected entry.

## Touch mode

A session-wide touch-mode flag suppresses only custom drawing, independently of logical selection and gesture state. Touch-down and native clicks (including clicks without touch-down) set it and invalidate the visible window. It persists through settling and page changes. Accepted wheel input, including a turn at a boundary, centre selection or an accepted Return release restores drawing, so the page Return goes back to shows its row; iPod's Home list ignores the flag and always draws its bar; synchronous centre-generated clicks bypass the native-click observer.

## Drawing

The canvas hook intersects the existing clip with the viewport and restores the clip, the LCD fill color and the LCD stroke color; this firmware's `canvas_save/restore` do not save those properties. The outline is one translucent fill (`0x402b2b2b`, a per-color alpha) plus two concentric one-pixel `canvas_stroke_rounded_rect` calls, a dark separator at radius 9 and the 70%-opaque white line at radius 8, with `bg_r = NULL`. The separator is the stock dark surface at `0xb32b2b2b`, so it disappears on the dark theme and only shows over bright artwork. It never calls `canvas_set_global_alpha`, so the shared alpha is untouched. A rounded call that reports failure (either stroke) or a row too small for the corner radius, keeps the square separator-plus-white outline instead.

iPod draws a selection bar instead of the outline. `widget_paint` (`0x65f28c`) calls `widget_on_paint_background`, then the widget's own paint, its children and `widget_on_paint_border`, so the background hook paints the bar behind the rows. The border hook then draws nothing, and the selection is loaded and settled once per frame, in the background hook. The bar spans the full surface width at the selected row's height and position, including the end bump; an entry narrower than half the surface, a grid tile, gets the bar in its own rectangle. It is a vertical gradient drawn as one-pixel `canvas_fill_rect` (`0x634ad0`) bands, which avoids the stock `gradient_t` ABI, followed by a one-pixel top highlight. The colors are the current accent's gradient and highlight, `ACCENTS` in `patch/offsets.inc` (Graphite by default: solid `#424242`, highlight `#555555`; see [Accent](#accent)). The bar uses the same clip, color restore and touch-mode rules as the outline. Rows only show it where they are transparent: after the iPod style edits, list rows, table rows, grid tiles (`s_btn_listblack`) and the settings rows (`s_btn_listitem`) are.

The iPod border hook draws the chevrons. Windows flagged `DRILL` in `patch/contexts.inc` get the stock `list_into` image on every row, over the bar as well: `widget_load_image` (`0x65c1ec`, served from the image manager's cache) once per paint, then `canvas_draw_icon` (`0x638c9c`: canvas, bitmap, centre x, centre y, relative to the canvas origin like the fills) per row, placed as stock rows place their `img_into`: left edge `CHEVRON_W` (58) from the row's right edge, inside the same viewport clip, which hides rows outside it. A playlist button spans the same 20 to 355 pixels as a category or album button, so the glyphs cover the same screen columns (317 to 327); Local Music's own images sit 5 pixels further right at a fixed x. On a drill window the row layouter lays out as if that image were the last child, so the title and a playlist's "more" button end before it. The widget's own window, found through its parents, decides, so a window painted during a transition keeps its own rows. Rows narrower than half the surface (tiles) get none, and none are drawn while `g_navbar_status` is set, as stock hides its own chevrons in multi-select.

The iPod border hook also draws the fast-scroll letter over the list. A step of more than one row (so the list has more than `SHORT_LIST_MAX` rows and the run has reached `LIST_FIRST_MS`) restarts a one-shot `LETTER_MS` (400 ms) timer. While it runs and the spin run is still past `LIST_FIRST_MS`, the surface draws the selected row's first non-space character, past an article on a library page ([Sorting](#sorting)). The text is the row's first non-empty `widget_get_text`, taken from the same `row_id` walk as the position-memory hashes, and a virtual table finds its logical row in the recycled pool; an offscreen or textless row draws nothing. The box is `LETTER_BOX` (80) square, `canvas_fill_rounded_rect` at radius `LETTER_RADIUS` (12) in the stock dark surface at `LETTER_ALPHA` (85%), or a square fill without a vgcanvas. The glyph is `LETTER_PX` (60) in the system default font (`canvas_set_font` `0x633e6c` with a null name), white (`canvas_set_text_color` `0x633d9c`), centred by `canvas_draw_text_in_rect` (`0x639524`) with the canvas alignment fields (`+0x34` horizontal, `+0x30` vertical) set to centre and middle. The clip, fill and text colors and alignment are restored afterwards; the font is not, since stock sets it before drawing its own text. The expiry repaints the live surface, which then draws nothing.

## Accent

iPod's accent ([ipod.md](ipod.md#display-settings)) replaces Shanling red wherever it is drawn:
the theme's style colors, inline `style:*` colors (a mutable style answers through the same
vtable), decoded images, and the payload's own selection bar and progress fill, which read
`ACCENTS` directly. `accent_map` (`patch/navigation.c`) is a pure function on one `color_t` (bytes
r, g, b, a). Stock uses `#FF1448`, `#7F0A24`, the pressed tint `#3D1920` and `#FF144840`, all
stock red blended with a neutral: each channel is `t * red + k`. Least squares against red with
the mean removed gives `t` (in 1/4096), then `k`. A color within `RED_TOLERANCE` (8) of that
blend, with `t` between 1/16 and 17/16 and `k` not below the tolerance, becomes `t * tone + k`
for one of the preset's tones (`ACCENTS`): the red tone for text colors (a property name ending
`text_color`, `tk_str_end_with`) and image pixels, the light tone for every other color property
and gradient stop; alpha is kept, and anything else, greys and other hues included,
is returned as it was. Anti-aliased edges onto black, white or a transparent background are such
blends, so they follow. No preset's colors are red blends, so mapping twice changes nothing.
Crimson returns every color unchanged, so its theme is stock.

- `style_get_color(color_t *ret, style, name, default)` returns its color through the hidden
  first argument. The hook runs stock through a trampoline and maps `*ret`, comparing the
  property name only when the color maps. Stock first asks `style_get_gradient` for every color
  (`bal` at `0x649fe8`; `style_data_get_gradient` turns a plain color into a one-stop gradient),
  so the gradient hook leaves the call that returns to `0x649ff0` unmapped, and the color hook
  alone picks the tone. The builder pins that `bal`.
- `style_get_gradient(style, name, gradient_t *out)` (`0x649f3c`) is a 12-instruction leaf with
  no GOT setup: null checks on the style and its vtable, then a tail call to the vtable's
  `get_gradient` (+0x18). The builder pins its first three words (`0x10800009, 0, 0x8c820000`)
  and replaces the first two with the jump; the payload repeats the checks, calls `get_gradient`
  through `$t9`, and maps the stops only when it filled the caller's `out`: `gradient_t` is 0x4c
  bytes (`gradient_init`), with the stop count at +8 and up to 8 `{color, offset}` stops from
  +0xc (`gradient_add_stop`, `gradient_get_first_color`). Widget backgrounds come this way.
- `image_manager_add(manager, name, bitmap_t *)` copies a freshly decoded bitmap into the cache.
  The hook maps its pixels first, only for plain theme image names (no `/` or `:`, so covers by
  path, `file://` or online URL are never touched), and only when the format
  is one of the 32-bit ones: `bitmap_t` has w at 0, h at 4, the format as a u16 at 0xe
  (1 RGBA, 2 ABGR, 3 BGRA, 4 ARGB in memory order, 4 bytes each by `bitmap_get_bpp_of_format`),
  its row stride from `bitmap_get_line_length`, and the pixels from
  `bitmap_lock_buffer_for_write`/`bitmap_unlock_buffer`. Premultiplied pixels are red blended
  with black, so they map the same way.

The confirm pop-up's discs (`confirm_ok`, `confirm_cancel` and their pressed images, names
starting `CONFIRM_IMAGE`) go through the same mapping with `CONFIRM_SURFACE` (`#2B2B2B`) as the
tone under every preset, Crimson included ([ipod.md](ipod.md#pop-ups)). The quick settings'
control discs (names starting `DROPDOWN_IMAGE`, `drop_`, except the brightness suns, `DROPDOWN_SUN`)
take the preset's red tone; when that tone is brighter than `GLYPH_LIGHT_MAX` (Graphite's silver),
an image that holds red also maps its neutral glyph pixels to `CONFIRM_SURFACE`, so the white glyph
turns dark ([quick settings](ipod.md#quick-settings)). Grey discs hold no red and are unchanged.
Any other image that holds red and an opaque pure-white pixel (a switch's knob, a disc's glyph; `#7F7F7F`
on a pressed disc) takes
the light tone instead of the red one, as do button backgrounds (names starting `BUTTON_IMAGE`,
`btn_`), whose white label is a widget's text; an image with red alone (a tick, the time page's
colon) keeps the red tone. Only Graphite's two tones differ, so only it scans for white.
The settings rows' category icons (`settings_icons` in `patch/ipod.json`, which the builder
writes to `SETTINGS_ICON_NAMES` in `stock.h`) are never mapped: several use stock red as their
category colour (Network Service, DLNA, Wi-Fi, Backlight), like the purple and orange ones.
`red_map` is the mapping with an explicit tone;
`accent_map` is it with the preset's tone, and the identity for Crimson.

Changing the accent saves it, sets the progress fill, calls `image_manager_unload_all(image_manager())`
(`0x645024`) and `widget_invalidate_force` on the window manager. Widgets ask for their style
colors, gradients and images on every paint, so the next frame decodes the images again through
the hook and shows every recoloured part; nothing needs a restart.

## Page transitions (iPod)

Stock V1.32 carries AWTK's window animators and uses one: `saverscreen_page.bin` has `close_anim_hint` `slide_left(duration=200,easing=cubic_out)`, the only hint in the stock assets. The window manager builds the animator named by a window's `anim_hint` (`window_animator_htranslate_create`, `0x683a08`, for `htranslate`) when it opens or closes. `navigator_to_with_context` (`0x4ec758`) opens a page above the rest and `navigator_back` (`0x4ec8e0`) is `window_manager_back`, so the page below stays alive and Return reverses the slide.

iPod sets the hint in its assets ([ipod.md](ipod.md#transitions)). `htranslate` animates two snapshots. `window_animator_prepare` (`0x682ee8`) paints both pages into offline canvases, through the payload's paint hooks, before `window_manager_is_animating` (`0x66daf4`) becomes true and while the sliding page is the top window; a hinted page is not painted before that, so its snapshot is its first paint. Each frame then blits the snapshots and paints only the status bar. The payload follows the snapshots in four places (`slides()`, `patch/navigation.c`). A surface whose own window is under a sliding top window draws the row it holds and nothing else: loading, recall and settling read the top page's state. A sliding page reveals its remembered row at once instead of by the glide, so it does not arrive mid-glide. Home's art is checked when Home itself is painted, not only when it is the top window. After a touch, a Return by button ends [touch mode](#touch-mode) on a 0 ms timer instead of at once: the closing page's snapshot, painted inside stock's handling of the release, keeps its row hidden, while the page behind shows its own.

## Status bar (iPod)

The background hook also handles top-level widgets, whose parent is the window manager. The status bar widget, which `system_bar_init` stores in the `system_bar` global (`0xa3a6d0`, a size-checked `CONTEXT_DATA` entry), gets one `canvas_fill_rect` in a darker color, `BAR_COLOR` (`#242424`), without a highlight, with the LCD fill color restored.

Painting the bar or the top window also updates `label_clock` in the bar with the device's local time: `time` and `localtime` (the stock libc imports, through the payload's GOT trampolines) give the hour and minute (`struct tm` `+8`, `+4`), written through `tk_snprintf` as `%d:%02d %s`, a 12-hour clock without seconds or a leading zero (`12:05 AM`, `6:14 PM`), then `widget_set_text_utf8`. A failed `time` (`-1`) or `localtime` (null), or an out-of-range field, shows `--:--`. The minute of the day (plus one, `~0` for `--:--`) is kept in the scratch page, so the label is looked up and written only when the minute shown changes; the bar repaints at least once a second through `systembar_showface`, so the clock turns over within a second, including after the screen wakes. Every page and dialog shows it. There is no volume flash: stock `on_wm_keyup_fun` opens `dialog/volume_dialog` on every volume turn (`0x4e8bb0`).

The same paints run `bar_sync`. `systembar_showface` (`0x52f610`) sets `img_bt`'s image every tick: a codec badge (`bar_sbc`, `bar_aac`, `bar_aptx`, `bar_aptxhd`, `bar_ldac`, by `bt_showcoding`) while Bluetooth is linked, otherwise `bar_bt` or `bar_btcon`. It also sets `img_battery`'s image (`bar_charge`, `bar_lowcharge` under 15%, else `bar_battery`), `label_battery`'s `%d%%` text and `progress_battery`'s value, and never the battery widgets' visibility. A badge that differs from the last one arms a `CODEC_MS` UI timer, whose steps set `widget_set_opacity` (`0x659f24`, the widget's `+0x34` byte, which `widget_paint` applies as the canvas global alpha) down and back up, switching the image to `bar_btcon` halfway. After that the bar's background paint, which runs before its children, puts `bar_btcon` back over each badge stock sets again, so no badge frame is drawn. A hidden `img_bt` or a non-badge image other than `bar_btcon` ends the flash. `bar_sync` then shows one battery widget with `widget_set_visible`, which returns early for an unchanged state and otherwise marks the parent for relayout (`widget_set_need_relayout_children`). `view_battery` is painted in the background hook from the level (`label_battery`'s text, since stock sets `progress_battery` to 0 while charging) and the charge state (`img_battery`'s image), and is invalidated only when those change.

## Now Playing (iPod)

`playing_page_init` (`0x52ca88`, standard PIC prologue, resumed at `0x52ca94`) registers the page's handlers, runs the page update (`0x52b6f4`) once and starts a 250 ms widget timer (`playing_timer_start`, `0x52c114`) that runs it again. The update finds each widget with a recursive `widget_lookup`, skips any it does not find, and gets the times from `player_playtime_and_length` (`0x514128`): elapsed and total seconds from `mclGetPlayTime` (`0x5ac078`), less a CUE track's start, elapsed clamped to the total, both 0 while stopped. It sets `slider_play` to value = elapsed, max = total, and formats both labels with `toolsTimeItoa` (`0x5bc410`): `%02d:%02d`, or `%02d:%02d:%02d` from an hour. `label_playlen` is its only reference to that name.

The hook runs the stock init, then caches `label_ipod_pos`, `label_ipod_album`, `label_ipod_remain` and the slider, and syncs them. It also caches `img_cover`, whose border paint (after the image) rounds its corners: for each of the `NP_ART_RADIUS` rows of a corner, one black `canvas_fill_rect` outside the arc and one edge pixel at the alpha it leaves uncovered, in 1/16 pixel from an integer square root. Painting the top window or the status bar syncs them again, as for the clock. The position is `MCL_POS + 1` of `deque_size(*mcl_pdeqplaylist)` ("3 of 12"), empty without a current record. The album is that record's `REC_ALBUM` (the record comes from `queue_now`, which Home's art shares), or, once `player_get_id3info` (`0x5146bc`) has parsed that record's file, the album it parsed: `g_play_id3_info` (`0xa4103c`, `0xa9c` bytes) holds the parsed path at `+0`, album at `+0x404` and artist at `+0x504` (`now_tag`). List play stops at the end of the selected queue, including Coverflow's folder plays. The checked branch at `0x5add50` in `mclNextSong` goes directly to its existing `mclStop` path at `0x5add90`, bypassing `on_player_autochange`. Jump folder is removed from Audio settings: the row-building loop at `0x4b9ef8` ends after IDs 0–13, excluding ID 14. Its stock startup calls remain valid, but `mclGetJumpFolder` returns 0 and `mclSetJumpFolder` stores 0, so a saved `FOLDERJUMP` value cannot enable it. Repeat one, Shuffle songs and Repeat all keep their selected behavior. Home's art uses the same parsed tags. The remaining time is the slider's max less its value, in stock's format with a leading minus, so it matches the elapsed label and follows a touch drag. Each label is written only when its source changes (position, queue length, album string pointer, or those seconds); `EVT_DESTROY` drops the cache. Stock's total label stays hidden.

The album-boundary test in `test/patch.py` executes stock `player_mainscheduling` → `mclAutoChange` → patched `mclNextSong` with Jump folder enabled. Equal-sized and one-track sibling folders stop without directory traversal, queue replacement or a decoder restart across repeated scheduler ticks. Explicitly loading the next album starts playback and updates the parsed metadata. Filesystem enumeration, tag decoding, image decoding and the audio decoder are mocked at their boundaries; the harness does not supply the resulting player metadata globals.

`set_repeatpoint`'s marker placement (`0x52a268`) puts the 3x10 A-B markers at x = 50 + t \* 290 / length, y 250. iPod's bar is 8 pixels at y 251, so it keeps y and patches the two immediates in `ipod.json` to its x and width. The stock slider paint fills `bg_color` and `fg_color` (`canvas_fill_rect` below a 4-pixel radius, `canvas_fill_rounded_rect` from it) before any image, so the image-less slider draws plain bars; iPod's `round_radius` of 4, half the bar, makes both a capsule. Its dragger is skipped when the `icon` style lookup is empty and `border_color` is transparent, and the fill then ends exactly at the value; the slider's style name has no theme entry, so no theme icon applies. `slide_with_bar` still seeks on a tap or drag anywhere on the bar.

**Volume.** The wheel stays on the volume, and stock opens `dialog/volume_dialog`, a full-screen transparent dialog holding `slider_vol` and `label_vol`. Its `highlight="default(alpha=200)"` made the window manager draw everything under it as a dimmed snapshot taken at open, so iPod's `volume_dialog.bin` drops that property (`volume_dialog` in `tools/ipod.py`) and nothing dims. When the paint hook paints that dialog, `vol_paint` draws the volume in the dialog's own paint. With Now Playing directly under it (the window manager's second-to-last child) it covers the band from `slider_play` to `label_playtime` in black, then draws the track (`TRACK_COLOR`, the slider's `bar_size`) and a white fill to `value / max`, both pills like the capsule, and "Volume N" centred in the times' row at `NP_TIMES_PX`. Over any other window, Quick Settings included, it draws a rounded `VOL_PANEL_*` panel in the fast-scroll letter's style (`FILL_RGB` at `LETTER_ALPHA`, `LETTER_RADIUS`) exactly over Quick Settings' brightness row (the build checks it), its rounded corners clear of the glass corners, with the bar as a pill on a `VOL_PANEL_TRACK` grey track (a non-zero fill at least a round dot) and just the number at its right, at `VOL_PANEL_PX`. `slider_vol` and `label_vol` are hidden, and `slider_vol` is moved onto the area drawn, so stock's partial repaints land on it. Those alone stopped after a tick or two on the device, so while the dialog is up a `VOL_POLL_MS` (50 ms) timer compares `slider_vol`'s value with the one last drawn and repaints the whole dialog when it changed; it removes itself once the dialog is no longer on top.

## Visualizer (iPod)

hciplayer's PEQ filter (`patch/peq_player.c`) taps what it plays, after `peq_process`, into `/tmp/q2vis` (tmpfs), a `vis_tap` (`patch/peq.h`): a ring of `VIS_RING` stereo float frames, the frames written (`seq`), the ring's rate and the `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` time of the last write. It keeps the first two channels, and above 48 kHz every `rate / 44100`th frame, so the ring holds about 1.5 s at any rate, room for hciplayer's 500 ms ALSA buffer (ao_alsa's `buffer_time` 500000 at `0x438a2c`, 16 periods). No lock: the writer stores the frames, then the rate and stamp, then `seq`, and the reader tolerates a torn frame. demo makes the file (`open`, `ftruncate`) under `/tmp/q2vis.tmp` and renames it, so hciplayer never maps a short file; hciplayer looks for it (`open`, `mmap64`) once a second of audio (frames counted, no clock call) until it appears. demo maps it read-write and bumps `want` each tick; after a second of audio with no bump the writer stops copying, and the next bump clears the ring, so a return never shows audio from before.

`ringnav_playing` adds the page (`visualizer_attach`, `patch/visualizer.c`): a `s_listitem_black` `list_item` appended to `slide_view` at run time, so a tap clicks it but a swipe still slides, and the page dots get `max` set to the new child count. The page only takes taps: the `slide_view` clips a page added at run time to a sliver when it paints it, so the visualizer draws in the paint hook of Now Playing's window instead, after all its children, clipped to the slide area and with the canvas origin moved there, and its Halo art is an `image` child of the window over the area's centre. That image takes `img_cover`'s image name and draw type, so stock's reload of the cover (load, set, unload) refreshes it too, and shows only while the Halo draws. Painting starts a `1000 / VIS_FPS` ms timer, which removes itself once Now Playing is not on top, the `slide_view`'s `value` is not the last page or the screen is off; each tick invalidates the slide_view. Nothing draws unless settled: no window animation, and the page's own paint, which runs just before the window's in the same frame and draws nothing, last found its canvas at the slide area's left edge (and hides the Halo art the moment it does not), so the page is not being dragged or sliding (`value` only changes once a slide ends); a tap leaves it there, and the timer skips repaints while a finger is down. 1.5 s after the audio stops (everything at rest, the style name faded) it stops repainting. Each tick takes `VIS_N` (1024) frames ending `VIS_LATENCY_MS` (480) behind the last write, moved on by the time since it: that buffer, which MPlayer refills a period at a time, less about a period. Bluetooth adds its own delay, so it stays the sync knob. It runs a Hann window and a radix-2 FFT over the mono mix and takes each of 64 log-spaced bands' (40 Hz to 16 kHz) loudest bin, 0 at `VIS_FLOOR_DB` below a full-scale sine and 1 at it (`vis_bands`). Bars rise fast and fall at `VIS_DECAY`, and caps hold `VIS_HOLD_MS`, then fall at `VIS_GRAVITY`. The VU needle is a one-pole follower of the window's RMS (tau 65 ms, 99% in 300 ms) on the meter's voltage scale, with 0 VU at `VIS_VU_REF_DB` dBFS; the LED lights at `VIS_LED_DB` dBFS peak. With nothing playing (`mclGetPlayStatus` not 2, or no write in a second) everything comes to rest. Drawing uses the stock vgcanvas entry points (`canvas_get_vgcanvas`, which clips to the canvas, then its path, fill and stroke calls; `float_t` is a float and `color_t` passes by value), translated to the slide area.

The tap sees only what the filter sees. Boot instantiates the filter only when PEQ was left on (`PLAYSET/EQFLAG`, see [Parametric EQ](#parametric-eq)). So with PEQ off the page's first timer tick (outside the paint) calls `peq_attach` (`patch/peq_ui.c`): what the editor's Off does, `peq_stock_eq`, which instantiates the filter in bypass, after making sure the active preset is saved bypassed, so a preset saved On while the stock flag is clear stays silent. Bypass is bit-exact, so 16- and 24-bit streams reach the output unchanged through the float conversion. DSD detaches the filter, so during DSD the page rests. Boot itself is left alone: a listener who never opens the page keeps the stock chain.

## Boot resume

Stock `home_page_init` resumes when Memory playback (`g_memory_play`) or car mode (`g_carmode`) is set. `memeory_startplayer` (`0x5164e0`) reads the saved state, rebuilds the queue into a new deque, sets the saved position with `mclSetStartSeekTime` when `g_memory_play` is 2 or in car mode, and returns the track index; it neither plays nor opens a page. Home then calls `navigator_to_with_context("playing_page", ctx)` (the `jalr` at `0x523de0`) with `ctx` `{queue, index, class, mode}`, mode 2 in car mode and 3 otherwise. `playing_page_init` passes `ctx` to `player_start` unless the class is `0xff`; `player_start` loads the queue, starts the player and pauses it (`mclSetPause`) unless the mode is 2.

Both builds make that `jalr` a checked `jal ringnav_boot`; the delay slot still stores the mode. `ringnav_boot` always passes mode 3 to `player_start`, including In-Vehicle mode, so the queue and track come back paused and Home stays; the saved time is restored in Location mode, while Track starts at the beginning. Home destroys its deque afterwards, as before, since `mclLoadPlayList` copies it. The page's two resets before `player_start` (key-seek mode and a countdown) are page state, set again when it opens. `hciplayer` touches `/tmp/notfile` or `/tmp/notsupport` for a missing or unsupported track; the window manager's `on_wm_timer_dialog` (`0x4ea784`) turns them into a `msginfo_dialog` toast on any page, and `playing_page_init` only deletes stale ones, so a bad restored track still shows its toast on Home.

The English stock labels are Memory playback: Off / Track / Location and
System Setting: In-Vehicle mode. Home's Now Playing action (`on_home_playing_onclick`
at `0x5237d0`) calls `on_backplay_click` (`0x4e805c`), which opens the player with
class `0xff`; this skips `player_start` and preserves the paused state. Play/Pause
resumes audio.

## Now Playing controls (iPod)

**Stock audit.** `playing_page` is not a navigation context, so a centre release (218, the power key) passed through to `on_wm_keyup_fun`, whose `screen_action` (`0x4f77d4`) turns the screen off, and a wheel release to the same handler, which changes the volume and opens `dialog/volume_dialog`. The page's own key-up handler (`0x52c248`) only knows Return and keys 222/223. Those drive stock's key seek: a long press sets `0xa3a6c0` (`0x529df0`), key-down (`0x52c754`) calls `playing_timer_clear` (`0x52c634`), sets the mode byte `0xa3a6c1` to 1, steps `slider_play` 5 seconds within `[0, max]` and writes `label_playtime`, and key-up calls `player_seek_time` (`0x51454c`) with the slider value, then `playing_timer_start` (`0x52c114`). A touch drag takes the slider's handlers from the page's `widget_foreach` visitor (`0x52ae6c`): value-changing (`0xf`, `0x52c950`) writes the label and, in mode 0, clears the timer and sets mode 2; value-changed (`0xe`, `0x52c5fc`) seeks the same way only in mode 2 (`0x52c364`). `player_seek_time` takes track seconds: it adds the current record's CUE start (`+0x48`), pauses DLNA, calls `mclSetSeek` (`0x5ab1ac`), then polls `player_playtime` every 10 ms, up to 2 s, until it reaches the target. `player_seek_start` (`0x5143a8`) is DLNA's and not used. `slider_set_value` emits will-change (`0xd`) and changed (`0xe`), never changing, so a set value in mode 0 neither seeks nor writes the label.

**Centre.** On the top `playing_page`, or its transparent `volume_dialog` directly above it, the key-up hook takes Centre. With no window animation or pressed pointer, the first release arms a `DOUBLE_CLICK_MS` timer. At expiry, `np_single` cycles the bottom band through Progress, Seek and Playback mode without changing playback. It closes an owned volume overlay so the selected control is visible. Seek shows a white position thumb and a centred Seek label between the times; Playback mode hides the slider/times and shows the current mode in the same band. A second release within the window cancels that callback and replays Centre to stock `on_wm_keyup_fun`, turning the screen off. Touch, animation, a pressed pointer, another top window or `EVT_DESTROY` cancel pending input. The page opens in Progress.

**Seek.** `np_wheel` moves the slider preview by `NP_SEEK_STEP` (5 seconds) within its native range, updates elapsed/remaining time and stops the native page timer while a seek is pending. `NP_SEEK_MS` (250 ms) after the last tick, `np_seek` calls `player_seek_time` once and restarts the timer. A changed queue or track, a hidden page, screen lock/off, touch or destruction rejects a pending seek. Timer allocation failure performs the seek immediately rather than leaving the native timer stopped. A changed preview sets `np_seek_changed`; the next single Centre press returns to Progress, even after the seek timer has settled. A fresh Seek visit clears that flag so an unchanged visit still advances to Playback mode. The slider retains stock touch dragging. The Progress wheel keeps its existing volume/lyrics behavior.

**Modes.** In Playback mode, the wheel wraps the four existing modes: List play, Repeat one, Shuffle songs and Repeat all. The mode's white text is centred in a flat black band, with separate mirrored white chevrons drawn at its ends by `paint_mode`; no extra panel or animation is added. Each selection calls stock `config_playmode(mode, 1)` to update and save the mode. List play stops at the queue boundary as described above; the other modes retain stock playback behavior. Seek and Mode own the wheel even with a stale volume popup directly above the player: the popup closes before the selected control handles the tick. While those controls are busy or a pointer is held, wheel input is consumed, never passed through to volume. Only Progress leaves the wheel on stock volume/lyric handling.

**Lyrics.** The page's 250 ms timer also keeps the lyrics in view: when `mclGetLyricSize()` is positive it finds the current line among `scroll_lrc`'s children (`0x52bcc8`), sums 25 px per wrapped 225 px row up to three lines before it, and calls `scroll_view_scroll_to(scroll_lrc, 0, y, 10)` whenever the view's y offset (`+0x84`, `SCROLL_Y`) differs, so a moved offset lasts one tick. `ringnav_playing` keeps `slide_view` and `scroll_lrc`. On the top `playing_page`, a wheel release while `slide_view`'s value is 1 (the lyrics page), `scroll_lrc` has children and `mclGetLyricSize()` is positive calls `playing_timer_clear` on the first tick, sets the offset `LYRIC_STEP` (25) px up or down within `[0, content height - view height]` with `scroll_view_set_offset`, rearms a `SCRUB_MS` timer and swallows the release. The timer or `np_cancel` from a touch, another top window or the page's `EVT_DESTROY` ends manual lyric scrolling; where the page remains active, `playing_timer_start` makes its first tick follow the current line again. Any other page, or no lyrics, leaves the wheel on the volume.

## Bluetooth AAC

When a headset opens the A2DP stream itself (AirPods taken out of the case), it picks AAC at 44.1 kHz. Stock `bluealsa` is still fed 48 kHz audio and drops about 8% of the AAC frames, so the audio is choppy until Bluetooth is toggled; a connection the Q2 opens picks 48 kHz. The build clears the 44.1 kHz bit in `bluealsa`'s AAC source capability, `c0 01 84 84 e2 00` at file offset `0x317b7` (object type, sampling frequencies, channels, VBR and bitrate). Octet 1 holds 8 to 44.1 kHz with 44.1 kHz in bit 0, so `0x317b8` goes from `01` to `00` and headsets pick 48 kHz, which every AAC sink must support. `BLUEALSA_SHA` pins the stock binary and `test/build.py` checks that this is the only changed byte.

## Clock

Stock loses the time zone across a power-off. Setting the time, by hand or from the network (`toolsSetRtcTime`, `0x5bc760`), runs `date -s` and then `hwclock -w`. BusyBox 1.31.1's `hwclock` writes local time unless `/var/lib/hwclock/adjtime` says `UTC`, and the rootfs has no such file, so the RTC gets the wall time of the `TZ` the player exported (`toolsSetTimeZone`). At boot the kernel (`hctosys`) reads the RTC as UTC and the player adds the time zone again, so the clock comes back off by the zone's UTC offset until the next network sync. The build changes the literal at `0x777928`, `hwclock -w` and two NULs, to `hwclock -wu`, so the RTC holds UTC as the kernel expects. An RTC last written by stock still holds local time, so the time has to be set once after updating.

## Parametric EQ

The PEQ adds two demo hooks: `playset_equalizer_page_init` (`0x4b642c`) builds the editor from `patch/peq_ui.c` (ten band rows, plus a spare past the last used band, up to 30), and `set_equalizer_value` (`0x4f9230`) keeps the stock equalizer filter instantiated, then sets `g_equalizer_flag` to the PEQ on/off state so the status-bar EQ icon (`systembar_showface`) follows it. The editor's ON/OFF writes the stock switch's `write_int_config(flag, "PLAYSET", "EQFLAG")`, which boot reads before instantiating the filter, so the state survives a restart. Frequency and Q open a value menu whose first row is an AWTK `edit` created at run time, keyed and styled as the stock playlist dialogs' (`kb_default_t9`, `ufloat`, `#2B2B2B` rounded), but with action text `done`: stock `edit_on_event` acts on the keyboard's OK (`EVT_IM_ACTION`) only for `done` (close the keyboard, then `EVT_VALUE_CHANGED`) or `next`, and the dialogs' `OK` relies on their own handlers, so with `OK` the value only reported on blur; its `EVT_VALUE_CHANGED` applies the text through the preset parser's number reader, clamped like Raise/Lower, and its `EVT_FOCUS` (`0x10e`) starts a 16 ms timer that finds the keyboard window under `window_manager()` and switches its `panel` pages to `symnum`, the number keys, trying up to 20 times since the keyboard can open a moment after the focus. Auto preamp is minus the peak of the combined response of the louder channel (1024 log-spaced points per channel, 20 Hz-20 kHz at 48 kHz). A band edit, including a gain picked from the wheel list, recomputes it only when the preamp was within 0.05 dB of Auto before the edit, so a picked or imported preamp stays put; nothing extra is stored. The editor handles Return (key 170) itself, since it replaces the page init that registered `on_common_keyup`. On the main and band screens a `PEQ_GRAPH_H` view under the title shows the draft's response (`response`, the same `|H|^2` as Auto preamp, one point per column, 20 Hz-20 kHz, ±`PEQ_GRAPH_DB`), drawn by `peq_paint` from the paint hook: a grey grid with 100, 1k and 10k labels, a 2-pixel vgcanvas line in the accent's light tone (white in Stock) with a fill that fades toward 0 dB, the other channel's line dimmer when left and right differ, a ring per band in use, the edited band's larger and filled; grey with no fill when PEQ is off. Rows show the caption on the left and the value right-aligned in grey; off bands and display-only rows are grey. The title names the preset loaded from **Presets**, and keeps the applied one's name until the active preset changes. In `/usr/bin/hciplayer` (modified MPlayer 1.3.0, pinned by `PLAYER_SHA` in `tools/peq.py`) the equalizer descriptor's open pointer at `0x893e0c` is redirected to `patch/peq_player.c`, loaded through the player's unused `PT_NULL` header at `0xe10000`. Stock skips the equalizer above 48 kHz (`slt` against 48001) in two places: the EQ-on command (`beqz` at `0x42ced0`, to the `===setequalizer off===` log) and the per-stream insert (`bnez` at `0x42fd84`, around the add-equalizer call `0x4291ac`). The build makes the first a nop and the second unconditional (`EQ_RATE_GATES` in `tools/peq.py`, pinned), so the filter runs at every rate; `peq_compile` accepts 8-384 kHz. DSD is the exception: it reaches the chain as integer PCM, possibly DoP, whose bits must arrive unchanged, so config returns detach (2) when the playing codec (`mpctx`, a pointer at `0xaf9e30`, `+0x2c` `sh_audio`, `+4` `codec`) has driver `dsdiff` (`+0x3e0`; the vendor's decoder for dsf, dff and SACD iso) or a name starting `ffdsd` (`+0x3d0`). The player re-adds the filter at each file load (`0x4224cc` sets the insert state back to pending), so the next PCM track is equalized again. The player reads the active preset from `/mnt/data/peq-active` on the playback loop, never in the PCM callback; `patch/peq.c` holds the shared parser, storage and biquad DSP.

A band's `enabled` is 0 off, 1 both channels, 2 left only, 3 right only (stream channels 0 and 1); `balance` (dB) attenuates the left above 0 and the right below, so it never adds clipping. APO `Channel:` sections set the scope of the filters after them, and per-side preamps become the louder side's common preamp plus `balance`. Out-of-range OFF filters, the blank slots some exporters write as `Fc 0 Hz … Q 0`, are skipped. Presets are `Q2PEQ02`; ten-band `Q2PEQ01` files load with `balance` 0 and default bands 11-30. The DSP runs bands only up to the last one in use, or still draining memory carried from the previous preset.

## Large MP3s

Stock `hciplayer` reads an MP3's Xing or VBRI header for its frame count, as MPlayer does, and then, when it found one, walks every frame of the file before playing (`0x4a2d5c`, called from `demux_audio_open` at `0x491de0`): it recounts the frames for the duration, stores the first `0x5000` frame offsets (about 8 minutes) in a table at `0xd30eb0` and sets MPlayer's `hr_mp3_seek` (`0xb152b8`). That reads the whole file off the card, 140 MB for a two-hour yt-dlp mix and 700 MB for a six-hour one, so the track starts late or seems not to start, and the gapless preload of the next track does it again. A seek past the table walks frames from its last entry, as slowly. An `Info` (CBR) header skips all of it.

The build makes the branch to that walk (`bnez` at `0x48fb3c`, `VBR_SCAN` in `tools/peq.py`, pinned) a nop, so a VBR file takes the path `Info` files already take: the duration is the header's frame count, `hr_mp3_seek` stays 0 and a seek goes to `movi_start + seconds * average bytes per second` at once. On its own that seek is as exact as the bitrate is even: on the card's files the worst case was 9 s in a 2.4-hour jazz mix and 6 minutes in a 5-hour game soundtrack whose tracks differ widely.

The Xing header's 100-point table fixes that. The audio demuxer's descriptor (`0x89fd90`) has its seek slot (`0x89fdbc`, `SEEK_SLOT` in `tools/peq.py`, pinned to stock `demux_audio_seek` at `0x48d1ac`) pointed at `mp3_seek` in `patch/peq_player.c`. For an MP3 (`priv->frmt` 1, `priv` at `demuxer+0xc70`) without `hr_mp3_seek` and not a factor seek, it reads the first frame once per file with `lseek64`/`read` on the stream's fd (`stream_t +0x14`) and restores the fd's offset, since stock's `stream_seek` can reuse its buffer without seeking. `mp3_toc` then parses the Xing header: MPEG 1, 2 or 2.5 Layer III, frame count and table both present; `Info` (CBR) and VBRI keep the stock seek. The target second (`priv->next_pts`, a double at `+8`, plus the offset for a relative seek) becomes a fraction of the track and is read off the table, interpolated between its points. Stock then seeks with `SEEK_ABSOLUTE | SEEK_FACTOR` to that fraction of `movi_start..movi_end`, and `next_pts` is set to the target second, so the shown time is exact. The landing is within the table's resolution, about 1% of the bytes between two points.

## Resume

`ringnav_sleep` (both variants, the UI thread) polls at most once a second. It reads the queue's playing record (`queue_now`) and `player_playtime_and_length` (`0x514128`): elapsed and total track seconds, CUE-relative, and -1 while nothing plays. A track is long when its total is at least `RESUME_MIN_S` (20 minutes), or it is under [Podcasts or Audiobooks](#podcasts-and-audiobooks), and its record has no CUE start or end (`+0x48`, `+0x4c`). Places are kept by the path's FNV-1a hash in a ring of `RESUME_SLOTS` (32), most recent first. The ring is read from `/mnt/data/ringnav-resume` on the first long track and written whole, to a `.tmp` then renamed. That happens every `RESUME_SAVE_S` (60) seconds of movement, once the time stands still for two polls (a pause), and on a track change. A place within `RESUME_EDGE_S` (30 s) of either end is forgotten. A track change waits one poll before it counts, so the previous track's time never belongs to the new one. When a long track with a saved place first plays (elapsed at least 1 s) with under `RESUME_START_S` (5 s) elapsed, `player_seek_time` jumps there, as Scrub commits. A track paused at 0 waits for Play, so Memory playback's paused boot does not seek, and its Location restore, already under way, is left alone. A hash collision only means a wrong resume point.

## Videos

**Videos** is the last Local Music row (`media_find` kind 5, `assets/icons/local_videos.png`, blue with a play screen), for a top-level `Videos` folder, case aside. It opens Books' page and list (`books_open(root, 1)`): `.mp4`, `.m4v`, `.mkv`, `.avi`, `.mov` and `.mpg` files, by path, one level of subfolders. A row (`play_video`) calls stock `player_stop` (`0x515b94`: hciplayer keeps its PCM, `hw:1,0`, open while paused), powers the DAC on with `mclSetDacPwr(1)` if `check_dacoff_state` turned it off (`g_dacoff_time` -1), then `fork`s and `execl`s `/usr/bin/q2video DEVICE PATH [VOLUME]`, the path a plain argument: nothing passes through a shell. `DEVICE` is `plughw:1,0`, the headphone DAC, whose hardware volume (`mclSetVol`, ioctl `0xc0044d00` on `/dev/shanling_dac`) stays the music's. When `mclGetOutputWay` is 1 (Bluetooth) it is `plug:bluealsa`, the PCM hciplayer opens for that way (`0x439858`, string `0x88ed30`): demo writes `defaults.bluealsa { device "XX:XX:..." profile "a2dp" }` for the connected device to `/mnt/data/asound.conf`, which `alsa.conf` loads, so the name needs no address. There the volume is hciplayer's soft volume: `device_set_volume` (`0x4f44d4`) calls `mclSetBtVol(v, v)`, sending `{mcl-setvol\%d}` with `v << 8 | v`, and hciplayer's handler (`0x42c770`) calls its `mixer_setvolume` (`0x428744`), which sets an `af_volume` level of `0.1 + 0.018 (v - 50)` from 50, `v * softvol-max (20) / 10000` below, and removes the filter at 100 (no `snd_mixer` is imported, so the soft path is the one taken). So the payload passes `g_volume` (`0xa38c41`, 0-100) as `VOLUME`, and the helper scales its samples by the same curve (`bt_gain`, in 1/65536) and leaves the DAC's mute alone. With a USB DAC (2) `DEVICE` is `plughw:2,0`: hciplayer opens `hw:2,0` for that way (`0x43975c`, string `0x88ed40`), and the plug converts the helper's 48 kHz stereo to what the DAC takes. Its volume is `device_set_volume`'s: `mclUsbAudioSetVol(v, v, "hw:1")`, or 100 when `g_usbvol_mode` (Audio settings' USB volume) is 0, fixed. That sets the DAC's own volume control; a DAC without one leaves `USB_MIXER` (`0xa3be6c`, no symbol) at -2 and sends hciplayer `{mcl-softvolflag\1}`, whose set-volume handler then applies the same `mixer_setvolume` curve as Bluetooth's. So `VOLUME` is plain `g_volume` (the helper's `bt_gain`) only for a variable volume on a DAC without its own control; otherwise it is `h` and the volume, which the helper shows on its bar and never applies.

While the helper runs, demo's loop goes on (timers, threads, the status bar) but `ringnav_input` drops every input event before the window manager, sending a key's release (`0x114`) to the helper as a datagram on its `AF_UNIX` socket `/tmp/q2video.sock` (`video_key`): Return `q`, Play/Pause `p`, the side buttons 222/223 `b` and `f`. The wheel is the volume, as on Now Playing: a tick steps `g_volume` as stock's `volume_dialog` keys do (`0x4a2044`: by 1, up only below 100 and `g_maxvolume`, down to 0), calls `device_set_volume(v, 1)` (the DAC's `mclSetLocalVol` ioctl, so the DAC follows at once, and for Bluetooth `mclSetBtVol`, so hciplayer has it after) and `write_int_config(v, "PLAYSET", "VOLUME")`, without stock's dialog, and sends `v` and the volume byte, at an end too. Centre toggles the wheel to seeking (`s`, then `b` and `f` per tick) and back (`v`); `video_poll` also puts it back on the volume `SCRUB_MS` (3 s) after the last tick or Centre. `ringnav_wm_paint` skips the window manager's paint. `video_poll`, from `ringnav_sleep`, calls `waitpid(WNOHANG)` every pass, meanwhile `reset_poweroptions_timer(1, 1, 1)` (`0x4f43dc`, the screen, standby and auto-power-off counters `on_wm_idle_status` and `check_to_poweroption` count down) and zeroes `g_dacoff_time`; at the helper's end it closes the socket and repaints the whole screen. Music stays stopped.

**q2video** (`patch/video.c`, about 10 KB) is linked by `tools/build.py` against the rootfs's own `libc-2.28.so`, `libpthread` and `libasound.so.2` (no MIPS sysroot: the file declares what it uses), `nan2008`, `fp64` and `/lib/ld-linux-mipsn8.so.1` as the stock binaries, with its own `__start` like glibc's. It runs stock `/usr/bin/ffmpeg` (FFmpeg 4.2): `-ss` the start, `-vf scale=375:320:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease:flags=fast_bilinear,format=bgra,pad=375:320:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2,transpose=clock` (the picture fitted into the landscape 375 × 320 view, then turned clockwise onto the portrait framebuffer, as the boot logo is stored), `-r 25 -f rawvideo pipe:3`, and `-ac 2 -ar 48000 -f s16le pipe:4`. `/dev/fb0` is 320 × 375, 32 bpp with red at bit 16 (ffmpeg's `bgra`) and two pages: `soc_fb.ko`'s `init_var_info` gives every 32-bit format red 16, green 8, blue 0 and `yres_virtual` = `yres` × `layer0_frames` (2 in `module_driver/soc_fb.sh`, `is_rotated=0`); stock `display_logo` (`0x400bf0`) refuses under 18 bpp and stores each pixel as the word `0xff000000 | R << 16 | G << 8 | B` (`0x400f2c`), the 320 × 375 JPEG unturned; demo's AWTK flush thread pans between the two pages (`FBIOPAN_DISPLAY` at `0x6265bc`, then `FBIO_WAITFORVSYNC`). The helper exits without drawing if `FBIOGET_VSCREENINFO` reports anything else. A thread writes the sound with `snd_pcm_writei` in 512-frame chunks (200 ms buffer) and keeps the frames heard (written less `snd_pcm_delay`); frame n goes to the framebuffer at n / 25 s of that clock, a whole frame late is dropped, and it draws the page demo is not showing and pans (`FBIOPAN_DISPLAY`), panning back to demo's page at the end. The video pipe is 4 MB (`F_SETPIPE_SZ`), so ffmpeg is paced by the sound. A `v` sets Bluetooth's gain to `bt_gain` of its volume. `s`, `b`, `f` and `v` show a bar for 1.5 s (`OVERLAY_MS`), drawn on the page after each frame's copy and before the pan, and redrawn with the next frame when paused: `BAR_H` (8) px high, `BAR_Y` (22) px above the picture's bottom and `BAR_X` (40) px in from its ends, clear of the 80 px glass corners, white for the volume of 100 or the position (start plus frames shown plus pending seek) of the length, black for the rest, by byte fills (white and black in BGRA). The length comes from a second `ffmpeg -nostdin -hide_banner -i PATH` started at once, its stderr read on the main loop's poll until it ends (or 4 KB) for `Duration: HH:MM:SS`; without one the position bar is not drawn. Pause holds the writer (the sound buffer plays out, then the clock stops); a seek restarts ffmpeg once 400 ms after the last tick, at the position plus 10 s per tick; without a sound stream ffmpeg fails at once and it runs again silent on a monotonic clock. After hciplayer's stop it opens the PCM (retrying 2 s; bluealsa takes one client per PCM, so this also waits for hciplayer's close), sets the DAC to PCM (`0xc0044d1b`, 0, as hciplayer does per PCM track, so a DSD track's mode is undone) and unmutes it (`0xc0044d1f`, 0); at the end it mutes it again, as a stopped hciplayer leaves it. ponytail: the frame rate is fixed at 25 and decoding is software, so sources much above the screen's size can fall behind; a smaller encode then plays smoothly.

Checks in `test/peq.py` (host build of `video.c`): ffmpeg's argv with and without sound, the frame pacing, Bluetooth's gain curve and sample scaling, the `Duration` parse, and the bar's pixels on a padded-line frame. `test/patch.py`: the Local Music row, the list, the child's `execl` argv after `player_stop`, the DAC power-on, Bluetooth's device and volume, silent USB, every key's datagram, the volume steps and their limits through `device_set_volume` and `write_int_config`, Centre's seek mode and its timeout, no other input or paint while playing, the held timeouts, and the end.

## Album artists

Stock has two artist settings. `artist_type` (`0xa38acc`, `PLAYSET ARTISTTYPE`, read by `read_artistype_value`) is the artist page's own switch (`btn_artistswitch`), which picks its albums and songs by album artist (`getAlbumByAlbumArtist`, `getMusicByAlbumAndAlbumSonger`) or by artist. `g_artist_type` (`0xa38ae4`) picks `GetAllAlbumArtist` for the Artists list and the album-artist queries for an artist's albums in `load_localclass_list`, but nothing in stock writes it, so Artists always grouped by the Artist tag and a guest artist split an album. `ringnav_localclass` copies `artist_type` into `g_artist_type` before every list load, so Artists, an artist's albums, the artist page and the [Queue menu](#queue-menu) follow one setting. `GetAllAlbumArtist`'s row callback (`0x4fbc28`) fills the name at `+0x18` as `getAllArtist`'s does, and also at `+0x24`, so the rows show and expand the same way. **Audio settings → Artists** (`ringnav_audioset`, after the remaining 14 stock rows in `scroll_view_playset`) toggles `artist_type` and writes `PLAYSET ARTISTTYPE`, as the artist page's switch does; the lists take it on their next load.

## Charge limit

The charger is a TI BQ25890 (`/sys/devices/.../bq25890_1_status`, read by `toolsGetChargeStatus`: 1 pre-charge, 2 fast charge, else 0). `switch_charge_enable(on)` (`0x4f3cfc`) sets GPIO `PE22`, its active-low /CE, through `toolsGpioSetValue` (libgpio, no shell); stock uses it only for USB mode's and AirPlay's charge choice (`g_usbdac_chargeflag`; `usbmode_page_init`, `usbmode_exit`, `airplay_page_init`). With /CE high the BQ25890's power path runs the player from USB and the battery holds. `get_battery_capacity` keeps `g_power_capacity` (0-100) and `g_power_chargestate`.

**System settings → Power management → Charge limit** (`ringnav_powermanager`, after stock's rows in `scroll_view_powermanager`; `Q2POD CHARGELIMIT` in `config.ini`). Every `CHARGE_POLL_MS` (5 s) `charge_poll`, from `ringnav_sleep`, stops charging at `CHARGE_STOP` (80%), stops it again if anything turned it back on meanwhile (USB mode's exit, AirPlay) while the charger reports charging, and hands it back at `CHARGE_RESUME` (75%) or when the limit is turned off: on, or `g_usbdac_chargeflag` while `usbmode_page` or `airplay_page` is open. ponytail: only while demo runs; a powered-off Q2 charges as stock.

## Low power

**System settings → Power management → Low power** (`Q2POD LOWPOWER`). The kernel (5.10, SMP) has CPU hotplug but no cpufreq core, so the clock cannot be lowered; Low power saves where it can without touching what you hear or see:

- **Second core.** While the screen is off and no video plays, CPU1 goes offline (`/sys/devices/system/cpu/cpu1/online`); the player decodes on one core, which plain playback, PEQ included, does not need two for. With the screen on, both cores are up, so Coverflow's software renderer and the UI never compete with hciplayer (`nice -5`, not real-time). The first offline of a boot is bracketed by `/mnt/data/q2pod-cpu1`: written and `fsync`ed before, unlinked and the directory `fsync`ed after. If a kernel stalled on it, the next boot finds the file, renames it `.bad` and never tries again (delete it to try again); a marker that cannot be synced means no offline that boot. An offline the kernel refuses stops the attempts until the next boot; a refused online is retried once a second.
- **Screen off.** The UI loop idles `LOW_OFF_SLEEP_MS` (100 ms) a pass instead of 40, about 10 passes a second; a key waits at most that.
- **Screen on, idle.** Once `LOW_IDLE_MS` (2 s) pass without input (`ringnav_input` times every event) or a window animation, each pass idles `LOW_IDLE_SLEEP_MS` (25 ms), about 30 a second instead of 125; the first input wakes it within that. Now Playing (`playing_page` on top) keeps stock's pace, since its visualizer, progress and lyrics run on timers an idle pass would make late.

Off, nothing above runs and no file is touched. ponytail: not measured on hardware; the core's saving depends on how deep the X2000's idle already goes.

## Coverflow sort

The **Sort** card, just before **Refresh library**, cycles **Album** (stock order), **Artist**, **Recently Added** and **Most Played**, saved in `/mnt/data/ringnav-coversort` (an int, through `blob_io`, so Refresh keeps it). The stock album list is still queried and kept as before (`cf.stock`); the shown list is a sorted copy of it. Artist and Recently Added rank albums by `toolsQueryDbTable(db, sql, row, 0)` with getAllAlbum's own row callback (`0x4fc324`) over the same `group by album COLLATE NOCASE` with another `ORDER BY`: `ifnull(max(year),0)=0, max(year), album` for Artist's year order and `max(time_create) desc` for Recently Added (the 0 skips stock's name sort, `0x5c8120`). Rows are matched back to the stock rows by album name, ASCII case aside, as the grouping is. Artist then sorts by artist without a leading article (`ringnav_sort_key`, as [Sorting](#sorting)), then that year rank. Most Played sums the [play counts](#play-counts) of each album's songs (`album_plays`, one pass over `getAllMusic`). Ties and albums without a rank keep the stock order; the Unknown card stays last. A press keeps the Sort card centred and queries only the ranking.

## Play counts

The Resume poll also counts listens, for every track but [Podcasts and Audiobooks](#podcasts-and-audiobooks). It adds the seconds heard: a step of 1 or 2 s between polls, so seeks, pauses and Resume's jump add nothing. Once a track longer than `LISTEN_MIN_S` (30 s) has had half its length, or `LISTEN_MAX_S` (4 minutes), heard, its path's FNV-1a hash (with a CUE track's start mixed in, as a CUE image's tracks share one path) is counted, once per play; a new key (another track, or another CUE track of the same image) or the time falling back under 2 s from past 30 s (repeat one, or a seek to the start) starts a new play. The counts are a fixed table of `PLAYS_SLOTS` (512) hash/count pairs, 4 KB, in `/mnt/data/ringnav-plays`, read on first use and written whole like Resume's ring. A counted track moves first; with no free slot, the furthest back of the lowest counts, the least recently played, is replaced. The file never grows, whatever the library's size. Keys are paths, so counts survive Update Local Music and a moved or renamed file starts again from zero.

**Most Played** (Local Music, under Shuffle Songs) runs `getAllMusic` through `staged` as Shuffle Songs does, looks each song's path hash up in the table and lists the top `PLAYS_TOP` (100) on a runtime `mostplayed_page` (`page_list`, as Books', in 64 px `page_row_detail` rows: the title over "Artist · N plays", or the count alone when untagged, in 16 px `#AAAAAA`, stock's `s_scrlabel_gray24l` grey), most played first and, among equal counts, the most recently counted first (the table's order). A row folder-plays that ranked `stSongInfo` deque from itself and leaves the play mode alone. A Centre hold opens the song menu over the same deque, as on Coverflow's tracks; for that the page is `CTX_FIXED`, so it also comes back on the last row. Nothing counted titles the page "No plays yet". The ranked deque is kept after the page closes, and a song counted while it is closed that is not in it is appended (up to 2 × `PLAYS_TOP`), so a later open ranks only those, not the library. The library is read again after Coverflow's `library_gen` moves (scan or delete), when the deque would grow past that or such a song is counted with the page open, or when a full table drops the count of a song in it, since the next best could be any song. A song first counted from stock's folder browser has that queue's record, without library tags, until then.

## Scrobbling

The same listen also appends a line to `/mnt/mmc/.scrobbler.log`, the card's root, in Rockbox's AudioScrobbler portable-player 1.1 format, so any `.scrobbler.log` uploader can send it to Last.fm or ListenBrainz. A missing file (`access`) gets the `#AUDIOSCROBBLER/1.1`, `#TZ/UTC` and `#CLIENT/Q2 Pod` header first. Each line is artist, album, title, track number, the length in seconds, `L` (listened), the start time and an empty MusicBrainz ID, tab-separated, from the record's `REC_ARTIST`, `REC_ALBUM`, `REC_TITLE` (the tag's; without one, `REC_NAME`, the file name, less its extension) and `REC_TRACK` (left empty when 0); tabs and newlines in tags become spaces. The start time is `time()` less the seconds heard, UTC since the clock fix keeps the system clock in UTC. Artist and album are the player's parsed tags once it has parsed the track (`now_tag`, see [Now Playing](#now-playing-ipod)), so a next folder's untagged records still log. A track with no artist, or a listen while the clock reads before 2020 (never set), is not logged, as scrobblers reject both. The file is only appended to, about 100 bytes a listen, by `scrobble_append` in `patch/scrobble.c` under the upload's lock.

**Upload Scrobbles** (Local Music, index 2, after Most Played, with a build-added copy of stock's 52 px `wifiset_wifi` as `local_scrobble`) is created only when `/mnt/mmc/.scrobble.ini`, read with `toolsReadConfig`, has `[LISTENBRAINZ] TOKEN` or all four of `[LASTFM] USER`, `PASSWORD`, `API_KEY` and `API_SECRET`. A press with `get_wifisignal` (`0x50e428`, the status bar's bars, -1 unless connected) at 0 or below says "Connect to Wi-Fi first". Otherwise `scrobble_start` reads the accounts again and starts one pthread, and a 500 ms timer (`upload_wait`) polls it and joins it, then toasts the count sent, "Nothing to upload" or "Upload failed, N sent". The thread reads the log, takes complete lines in batches of 50 (Last.fm's limit) and skips comments, lines not rated `L` and lines without artist, title or time. It sends a batch through demo's own libcurl imports: ListenBrainz `submit-listens` as `import` JSON with `Authorization: Token`, and Last.fm `track.scrobble`, form-encoded and signed (`api_sig`, the MD5, from demo's libcrypto `MD5_*` imports, of the sorted names and values followed by the secret), with a session key from `auth.getMobileSession` fetched once per upload, before the first batch. A batch counts as sent only when every configured service answered 200 (and Last.fm's reply has no `error`). The first failure stops the upload. A service that took a batch the other refused gets it again next time. Then, under the lock, the bytes of every sent batch go to `.scrobbler.log.sent` (comments dropped) and the log is rewritten, to a `.tmp` then renamed, as the header plus what follows them. Listens appended during the upload are therefore kept. Stock's HTTPS (Tidal, Baidu) sets `SSL_VERIFYPEER` and `SSL_VERIFYHOST` to 0, as the rootfs has no CA bundle. The upload does the same, unless `/mnt/mmc/.scrobble.pem` exists, in which case it is `CAINFO` with both checks on.

## Battery

The kernel has no cpufreq core (no governor to set) and stays stock; three rootfs drains change instead.

- **drop_caches.** `check_mem_thd` (`0x512050`, started by `platform_init`) writes `3` to `/proc/sys/vm/drop_caches` every sixth second for the life of the player, throwing away the page, dentry and inode caches: the library database, the card's directories, the playing file's readahead and squashfs assets are read again. `toolsGetSysMemFree` has no callers, so nothing depends on it. The `beq` at `0x5120a8` that skips the write when the open failed becomes `b` (`DROP_CACHES` in `tools/build.py`); the thread keeps its one-second sleep and never writes.
- **UI loop.** `main_loop_sleep_default` sleeps at most 8 ms (its expected-sleep argument can only shorten that), so AWTK's loop runs at 125 Hz, screen on or off. `ringnav_sleep` first sleeps `SCREEN_OFF_SLEEP_MS` (40 ms, `patch/offsets.inc`) with `sleep_ms` (`0x724928`) while `g_backlight_status` is clear; stock then finds its 8 ms gone, sleeps 0 and updates its own timestamps. The loop runs at about 20 Hz with the screen off, and a key pressed then waits at most 40 ms longer. The first pass with the backlight back on calls `widget_invalidate_force` on the window manager once, so the whole screen repaints on wake rather than only the regions that change next.
- **Watchdog.** `platform_init` starts `checkappprocess.sh`, which forks `pgrep` every 2 s and reboots if `demo` is gone. Its `sleep 2` becomes `sleep 10` (`WATCHDOG` in `tools/build.py`, pinned by SHA-256), so a crashed player reboots within 10 s.
